Velocity-gauge NABEC vs finite-difference APT for water

For an insulator the NABEC is the ordinary atomic polar tensor, so the Dreyer Eq. 7 result must match the finite-difference dipole derivative. With plane-wave pseudopotentials and a large enough box it does, to below 10⁻³ e. The GTH runs further down show why it first looked otherwise. LDA, cubic box of side L, Ecut in Ha. Charges in e; tensors Z[α, β] with α = dipole direction, β = displacement.

The Γ-point velocity gauge needs flat occupied bands, which for water takes L ≳ 16–20 bohr; the length gauge converges far earlier. With GTH (all settings below), O projectors are so hard in momentum space that velocity-type quantities do not converge up to 80 Ha and oscillate, which masked the box dependence.
